V / Reference handbook

Read the record.
Know its limits.

Classification describes potential consequence. Contract status describes a relationship. Neither is a guarantee of safety.

Draft lore: this guide develops the supplied fictional setting. New institutions, procedures, and interpretations are proposed worldbuilding, pending author approval.

Threat classifications

The supplied records use six labels without defining their order. This draft proposes Omega as the highest tier, followed by Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon. The hierarchy is an editorial proposal, not an established rule from the original files.

OMEGA

Systemic consequence

May affect institutional continuity, major jurisdictions, or the foundations of a covenant. Review authority and implications across departments.

ALPHA

Major consequence

May compromise a protected collection, a significant contract, or a major site. Requires a named lead and review of the applicable terms.

BETA

Substantial consequence

Can disrupt a department or sustain a recurring contractual burden. Maintain a dedicated record and reconcile outstanding obligations.

GAMMA

Localized consequence

Can affect a bounded location or a limited set of records. Preserve custody and note any change in scope.

DELTA

Situational consequence

May cause serious harm under particular encounter conditions. Identify those conditions rather than assuming a routine response.

EPSILON

Limited consequence

Typically has a narrow operating scope. Small losses can still accumulate or undermine a larger procedure.

Why a hostile entity can have a lower tier

Threat and disposition answer different questions. The Thorn-Crown Revenant is hostile but tied to an unsettled grievance; the dormant Canopy Leviathan may have much wider potential reach. A friendly or dormant status does not lower the possible consequences of a failure.

Contract and custody statuses

Active Binding
An operative covenant is recorded. Its scope still needs to be read.
Contracted / Retained
A service relationship exists. Contracted implies a defined assignment; retained implies availability under agreed terms.
Contained / Detained
Movement or access is restricted. Detention does not establish a permanent resolution.
Secured
Custody of an object is recorded. Security depends on the stated conditions of storage.
Dormant
No current activity is recorded; future activation remains possible.
Hostile / Restless
An adverse disposition or unresolved disturbance is recorded. This does not by itself define jurisdiction.
Managed / Monitored
Managed indicates an ongoing intervention; monitored indicates observation without claiming control.
Unmanaged
No effective management arrangement is recorded.
Unlocated / Rumored
Neither custody nor location is established. Keep reports distinct from verified accessions.
Restricted Access
Consultation is limited. This is an access designation, not proof of physical containment.
Compound statuses
Read every component. “Neutralized/Detained” preserves both supplied labels; the archive should not infer permanent destruction from that wording.

Reading a dossier

The original archival assessment and binding seal appear first. Expanded appearance, abilities, limitations, protocols, and encounter fragments are clearly marked as proposed fiction. A protocol is an in-world narrative procedure, not real-world advice.

Missing information remains missing. Records 17–33 did not contain binding seals in the supplied detail database, so their pages do not manufacture one. A later author-approved addition can fill that gap without pretending it was always present.

Evidence and uncertainty

Within the fiction, separate a witnessed observation from an interpretation. Outside the fiction, religious traditions, historical claims, and cryptid stories require their own sources. The lore library remains a preserved set of unverified project notes; it is not a scholarly reference.
