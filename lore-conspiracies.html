Conspiracies and mysteries

UFO and UAP disclosure timeline, 2017 to 2026

Official government record on unidentified anomalous phenomena.

Origin / date: US government releases and hearings, 2020 to 2026 in the research brief.

Plainly: official findings document unexplained sightings. They do NOT document extraterrestrial craft or contact. Claims of recovered craft, including Grusch's, remain uncorroborated. The brief has no entry dated 2017, so the timeline below starts in 2020.

Record status: Documented (sightings); Claimed (alien craft)

Latest: Pentagon file releases began May 8, 2026.

  • Apr 27, 2020: the Navy officially released three UAP videos; they remain classed as unidentified.
  • Jun 25, 2021: ODNI Preliminary Assessment reviewed 144 reports; 1 was explained (a balloon) and no firm conclusions were reached.
  • Jul 26, 2023: David Grusch told a House Oversight hearing about recovered craft and 'non-human biologics'. His claims were uncorroborated and the Pentagon disputed them.
  • Mar 8, 2024: AARO Historical Record Vol. 1 found no evidence that any government-investigated UAP was extraterrestrial technology, and no empirical evidence of reverse-engineering programs.
  • FY2026 NDAA: the UAP Disclosure Act was reportedly left out, with narrower briefing provisions only (secondary reports).
  • Feb 19, 2026: a presidential social-media directive about releasing UFO files appeared; whether a signed order exists is unverified.
  • May 8, 2026: the Pentagon began publishing UFO files online, 162 records in the first batch, with further batches since.
  • About Jul 21, 2026: AARO's FY2025 annual report counted 319 new reports, with 114 resolved as ordinary objects; a Navy-reported event off Virginia is under investigation.

Area 51

Secret US Air Force test site in Nevada.

Origin / date: Established in the 1950s.

The CIA's 2013 release of 407 pages acknowledged Area 51 as a test site for the U-2 and OXCART aircraft. The documents say nothing about aliens. Secrecy and unusual aircraft fed the legends.

Record status: Documented (test site); Claimed (aliens)

Latest: CIA 2013 release, 407 pages.

Roswell, 1947

New Mexico crash that became a flying-saucer legend.

Origin / date: July 1947, near Roswell, New Mexico.

The Air Force reports of the 1990s attribute the debris to Project Mogul, a balloon program for monitoring Soviet tests. Exact report dates are unverified in the brief. Later alien-autopsy stories have been discredited.

Record status: Documented (debris); Disputed (alien claims)

Men in Black

Mysterious suited visitors said to silence UFO witnesses.

Origin / date: Popularized in the 1950s and 1960s UFO literature.

Stories describe dark-suited men who warn witnesses to stay quiet. No documented agency of this kind exists, and the accounts are anecdotal. The idea later became a film franchise.

Record status: Claimed

Ancient astronauts

Idea that extraterrestrials shaped early civilizations.

Origin / date: Popularized in the 1960s and 1970s.

Proponents point to monuments and myths as evidence of visitors. Archaeologists have documented how human societies built these works, and the theory is widely rejected. It also tends to undervalue the achievements of ancient peoples.

Record status: Disputed

Moon-landing hoax claim

Claim that the Apollo landings were staged.

Origin / date: Appeared in the 1970s.

The Apollo landings between 1969 and 1972 are thoroughly documented by independent evidence, including returned samples and retroreflectors left on the surface. The hoax claim has been debunked point by point.

Record status: Debunked

Flat Earth

Belief that the Earth is a flat disc.

Origin / date: Modern movement dates to the 19th century.

Observations, spaceflight, navigation and basic physics show Earth is a globe, as understood since ancient Greece. The movement is a small fringe that has grown through social media.

Record status: Debunked

Bermuda Triangle

Atlantic area said to swallow ships and aircraft.

Origin / date: Named in 1964 magazine writing.

Insurance and coast guard data do not show unusual losses in the region compared with other busy waters. Storms and navigation errors explain many cases, and some stories are exaggerated.

Record status: Disputed

Philadelphia Experiment

Alleged 1943 Navy test making a ship invisible.

Origin / date: Story emerged in the 1950s.

The Navy says no such experiment took place. The tale grew from letters sent to an author and from misunderstood degaussing work, which reduced ships' magnetic signatures.

Record status: Disputed

MK-Ultra

CIA program of mind-control experiments.

Origin / date: 1953 to the 1960s; exposed in 1975 and 1977 hearings.

This one is real. The CIA ran covert experiments, including with LSD, on unwitting people. Most records were destroyed in 1973, and surviving files were declassified and examined in 1975 to 1977 congressional and presidential inquiries.

Record status: Documented

Latest: Declassified 1975 to 1977.

Project Blue Book

US Air Force study of UFO reports.

Origin / date: 1952 to 1969.

This is a real program. It reviewed thousands of sightings, with most explained and a portion listed as unidentified. The Air Force ended it in 1969, concluding no threat to national security.

Record status: Documented

Mandela Effect

Shared false memories of details.

Origin / date: Named around 2009 after claims of misremembering Nelson Mandela's death.

Many people recall the same wrong detail, such as spellings or movie lines. Psychologists explain this through memory reconstruction and suggestion. Nelson Mandela died in 2013.

Record status: Explained by psychology

Simulation hypothesis

Idea that reality may be a computer simulation.

Origin / date: Formalized by philosopher Nick Bostrom in 2003.

It is a philosophical argument rather than a conspiracy. No test has confirmed it, and many scientists and philosophers dispute its premises.

Record status: Speculative

Hollow Earth and Agartha

Legend of vast hidden realms beneath the surface.

Origin / date: Hollow-Earth ideas date to the 17th century; Agartha entered Western occult writing in the 19th century.

Seismic data show Earth has a solid mantle and a layered core, so no hollow interior exists. Original project notes described maglev tunnel networks, which have no evidence behind them. It remains a fixture of fiction.

Record status: Debunked

New Jersey drone sightings, 2024

Wave of reported drones over New Jersey.

Origin / date: Late 2024.

A DHS and FBI joint statement on Dec 12, 2024 said no threat was found, and many sightings were manned aircraft. Public concern remained high during the episode.

Record status: Documented (statement); Disputed (origin claims)

Latest: DHS and FBI statement, Dec 12, 2024.

Skinwalkers (Yee naaldlooshii)

Figure from Navajo (Dine) tradition.

Origin / date: Dine oral tradition.

In Navajo belief, skinwalkers are harmful people who take animal forms. Many Dine consider the subject sensitive and prefer it not be discussed casually. This archive treats it as folklore and as part of a living culture.

Record status: Folklore

Skinwalker Ranch

Utah property known for paranormal claims.

Origin / date: Reports since the mid-20th century.

The ranch was sold in 2016 to Adamantium Holdings (Brandon Fugal) and has been a research site for a TV series. Its current status is unverified. It is not connected to the Navajo tradition of the same name, and no claim there has been verified.

Record status: Claimed

Latest: Sold 2016; current status unverified.

Cryptids

Loch Ness Monster

Reported creature of a Scottish loch.

Origin / date: Modern fame from 1933; a 6th-century tale about St. Columba is often cited.

The August 2023 'Quest' was the largest search in decades, using hydrophones and sonar, and found nothing conclusive. The famous 1934 'surgeon's photograph' was admitted to be a hoax.

Record status: Claimed (no verified evidence)

Latest: Aug 2023 Quest found nothing conclusive.

Bigfoot (Sasquatch)

Reported ape-like creature of North American forests.

Origin / date: Name popularized in 1958; Indigenous stories of wild beings are far older.

No body or confirmed remains exist. Tracks and videos have been explained as hoaxes or misidentifications, including the 1967 Patterson-Gimlin film, which remains debated.

Record status: Claimed

Yeti

Reported snow creature of the Himalaya.

Origin / date: Western reports from the 19th and 20th centuries.

A 2017 DNA study of purported samples found they belonged to bears. Local lore of wild mountain beings predates Western interest.

Record status: Disputed

Chupacabra

Reported livestock-attacking creature.

Origin / date: First reported in Puerto Rico in 1995.

Descriptions changed over time. Many carcasses described as chupacabras turned out to be dogs or coyotes with mange.

Record status: Disputed

Mothman

Winged figure reported near Point Pleasant, West Virginia.

Origin / date: 1966 to 1967.

Witness reports of a large winged creature coincided with the Silver Bridge collapse on Dec 15, 1967, which killed 46 people. Investigators attributed the collapse to a metal eyebar failure. The link to Mothman is folklore.

Record status: Claimed

Jersey Devil

Winged creature of New Jersey Pine Barrens folklore.

Origin / date: Traced to 18th century legend; mass reports in 1909.

The tale of the 13th child of Mother Leeds is folklore. The 1909 sightings wave was widely reported, and some elements were hoaxes. The NHL's New Jersey Devils take their name from it.

Record status: Folklore

Thunderbird

Giant bird of many Native American traditions.

Origin / date: Indigenous oral tradition, varying by nation.

In several nations, the Thunderbird is a powerful spirit linked to storms. It is a sacred figure and not simply a monster. Modern 'giant bird' sighting claims are unverified.

Record status: Folklore (Indigenous); Claimed (modern reports)

Flatwoods Monster

Entity reported in West Virginia.

Origin / date: Sept 12, 1952, Flatwoods.

Witnesses described a tall figure with a spade-shaped head. Skeptical investigators suggested a meteor and a barn owl, which remains debated.

Record status: Claimed

Dover Demon

Creature reported in Massachusetts.

Origin / date: April 1977, Dover.

A small number of teenagers reported a thin, large-headed figure on separate nights. No physical evidence was found.

Record status: Claimed

Wendigo

Spirit in Algonquian-speaking peoples' traditions.

Origin / date: Oral traditions of the northern woodlands.

In these traditions, the wendigo is tied to winter, hunger and the danger of greed. It is part of living cultures, and the term 'wendigo psychosis' is contested by scholars. Treat it with respect, not as a monster.

Record status: Folklore

Kraken

Giant sea monster of Scandinavian legend.

Origin / date: Described in 18th century Norse writing.

The legend likely drew on sightings of giant squid. The giant squid was first filmed alive in its deep-sea habitat in 2012.

Record status: Folklore with real-animal basis

Mokele-mbembe

Reported creature of the Congo Basin.

Origin / date: Western reports from the early 20th century.

Some accounts compare it to a sauropod dinosaur. Expeditions have found no evidence, and scientists regard it as unsupported.

Record status: Claimed
